    Definition 6.10.

    expected value
    🔗
    For example, imagine you have a coin that comes up heads \(1/4\) of the time and tails \(3/4\) of the time. If you gain \(2\) dollars for every heads and lose \(4\) dollars for every tails, the expected value of the game is
    \begin{equation*} (\frac{1}{4}*2) + (\frac{3}{4} * -4) =\frac{1}{2} -3 = -\frac{5}{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • If we roll a fair \(6\) sided die and win dollars equal to the number rolled, what is the expected value of the game?
      🔗
    • If we roll two fair \(6\) sided dice and win dollars equal to the sum of the rolled numbers, what is the expected value of the game?
      🔗

    Definition 6.12.

    One-card Stud Poker We begin with a deck of cards in which \(50\%\) are Aces (you can use Red cards for Aces) and \(50\%\) are Kings (you can use Black cards for Kings). There are two players and one dealer. The play begins by each player putting in the ante (1 chip). Each player is dealt one card face down. WITHOUT LOOKING AT HIS OR HER CARD, the players decide to Bet (say, 1 chip) or Fold. Players secretly show the dealer their choice. If one player bet and the other folded, then the player who bet wins. If both bet or both fold, then Ace beats King (or Red beats Black); winner takes the pot (all the chips from the ante and any bets). If there is a tie, they split the pot.
    🔗

    Definition 6.34.

    Undercut is a two player game between Rose and Colin. Each round, both players choose a number between \(1\) and \(5\) and simultaneously reveal their decisions. Let their decisions be \(a\) and \(b\) with \(a \leq b\text{.}\)
    🔗
    • If \(a+1 = b\text{,}\) then the person who played \(a\) adds \(a+b\) to their score and the opponent gets nothing.
      🔗
    • Otherwise, each player adds their own number to their score.
      🔗
    For example, if Rose plays \(5\) and Colin plays \(3\text{,}\) Rose gains \(5\) points and Colin gains \(3\) for a net difference of \(+2\) for Rose. But if Rose plays \(5\) and Colin plays \(4\text{,}\) Rose gets \(0\) points and Colin gets \(9\) for a net change of \(-9\) for Rose!
    🔗
