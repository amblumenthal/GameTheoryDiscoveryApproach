\begin{align*} \begin{bmatrix} (1,-2) \amp (0,2) \\ (-1,3) \amp (3,-1) \end{bmatrix} \end{align*}
  1. Determine Rose's Payoff Matrix. And Colin's Payoff matrix.
  2. Think back to von Neumann. If we want our opponent to have no incentive to change strategy, what should be true about our opponent's expected values?
  3. Find a strategy for Rose that equates Colin's results. (Be careful which payout matrix you use.)
  4. Find a strategy for Colin that equates Rose's results.
  5. If both players take the strategies you've determined, does either player have incentive to change their strategy?
